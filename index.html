02 // BEHAVIORAL FOUNDATION

System 1 vs. System 2 Thinking

Applying Daniel Kahneman's Dual-Process Cognitive Model to Financial Decisions.

System 1: Fast, Intuitive, Emotional

The Trader's Trap
  • ✕ Instinctive Reaction: Operates automatically with little or no conscious effort.
  • ✕ Survival Heuristics: Evolutionary fight-or-flight triggers panic-selling during market drawdowns.
  • ✕ Social Proof: Urges buying when everyone else is euphoric (FOMO at market peaks).
  • ✕ Pain Avoidance: Treats a paper loss as unbearable, refusing to realize it until capital is destroyed.
Default State
In fast-moving markets, System 1 hijacks decision-making, converting rational strategy into emotional gambling.

System 2: Slow, Deliberate, Probabilistic

The Quantitative Mind
  • ✓ Calculated Probabilities: Focuses on expected value: EV = (Win% × Avg Win) - (Loss% × Avg Loss).
  • ✓ Pre-Commitment: Fixes stop-losses and position sizes before entering a trade.
  • ✓ Emotional Detachment: Treats individual trades as mere samples in a long-term statistical distribution.
  • ✓ Systematic Execution: Executes rules regardless of current fear or greed levels.
The Antidote
Consistent profitability requires building systematic rules that insulate trading execution from System 1 impulses.

09 // SYSTEMATIC RISK MANAGEMENT

The Antidote: Quantitative Rules

Constructing rule-based barriers to insulate execution from emotional bias.

1. The 1%–2% Rule

Capital Preservation

Never risk more than 1% to 2% of total account equity on any single trade.

Shares = (Equity × 0.01) / (Entry - StopLoss)

Even a catastrophic string of 10 consecutive losses preserves >90% of your capital.

2. Asymmetric R:R

Positive Expectancy

Target a minimum of 2:1 or 3:1 Reward-to-Risk ratio on all trade entries.

Expectancy = (Win% × 3R) - (Loss% × 1R)

With a 3:1 ratio, you can be wrong 60% of the time and still remain consistently profitable.

3. Automated Stops

Pre-Commitment

Hard stop-orders placed simultaneously with order entry eliminate loss aversion.

Never adjust a stop-loss downward during market panic.

Removes decision-making from System 1 during high-stress market moments.
